What A_L_I_S_E is, why it publishes structural analysis, and how to read its articles.

What is A_L_I_S_E?

A_L_I_S_E is a publication that interprets the structures beneath visible events through Universal OS (A_L_I_S_E), an evolving approach formed through long-term collaboration between a human and AI.

Its topics include politics, economics, society, technology, culture, institutions, human behavior, and everyday observations. Rather than stopping at the headline, it examines shifting conditions, constraints, interests, and relationships.

Why publish the analyses?

The first aim is to offer readers a different way of seeing familiar events: not merely what happened, but what may be operating underneath.

The second is to explore what collaborative structural analysis can capture and overlook when Universal OS is applied to varied real-world subjects. Publishing the work is part of that continuing practice.

Articles are interpretations open to checking against original sources. Publication alone does not establish the scientific validity or predictive performance of the approach.

How to read the articles

SCAN examines emerging events or signals through a structural lens.

MEMO collects shorter reflections and questions. Both series retain their own numbering, titles, publication dates, and canonical note links.

Human judgment guides topic selection, editing, and the final publication decision; AI contributes to dialogue and analysis. Universal OS names an evolving analytical framework, not an independently responsible author.

How this relates to S. Meta

S. Meta Research Archives preserves public research, concepts, and source paths for inspection. S. Meta on note explores the questions and motivations behind that research through essays.

A_L_I_S_E has a different editorial role: it applies Universal OS to real-world structural analysis rather than rewriting research papers in simpler language.

What is published here

The index links to 50 verified articles: SCAN #01–#42, MEMO #01–#06, and two independent guide articles. Article bodies are published on note in Japanese. Titles, dates, and URLs are also available in machine-readable data.

For new readers, start with the profile article or the five-article reading guide (both in Japanese).
